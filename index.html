THE RULE

You do not own the thing you discover. You can earn platform attribution for the first complete, verifiable contribution that makes it useful here.

First proven.
Not first shouted.

01 / FIND

Bring something real.

A public source, photo, primary document or other lawful evidence. A vague tip cannot reserve a discovery.

02 / PROVE

Make a falsifiable claim.

Say exactly what is unusual, new or under-recognised and provide enough evidence for another person to challenge it.

03 / ATTRIBUTE

Keep the receipt.

Accepted contributions receive a public timestamp and attribution. Later contributors can extend the record rather than erase the first useful proof.

Designed against bullshit.

No pay-to-claim.A user never needs to stake money for the chance of earning. Submission is free.
No ownership fiction.Attribution on Claim Zero does not create ownership over a third-party company, creator, product, property or IP.
No guaranteed income.A claim may create zero economic value. Any future revenue share must be governed by explicit terms before monetisation.
Challenge is a feature.Anyone may bring prior art or stronger evidence. The record should become more accurate, not more flattering.
Illustrative claim types — not live claims

What counts?

product

A local product with overlooked demand.

Evidence must establish the product, maker and why the specific commercial signal is not already obvious.

Not: “I found a famous restaurant.”
creator

Exceptional work before broad distribution.

The creator remains the owner of their work. Your claim is only the documented discovery contribution.

Not: scraping private profiles.
demand

A measurable unmet need.

Bring data showing demand and a genuine supply gap. Opinions without evidence are not discoveries.

Not: “people would probably buy this.”

Legal. Moral. Procedural.

Alpha status. Claim Zero is an experimental public prototype, not an investment product, broker, employment marketplace, lottery or promise of payment.

Lawful evidence only. Do not submit confidential business information, hacked/leaked material, private personal data, medical/financial records, doxxing, trade secrets, content obtained by trespass or surveillance, or material you do not have a lawful basis to share.

Rights stay where they belong. A discovery record does not transfer ownership of a person, company, product, artwork, trademark, patent, property or copyright. Contributors grant only the permission necessary to display and evaluate material they submit.

Economic claims require separate terms. No page on this alpha creates a right to royalties, securities, equity or future income. If a discovery is later monetised, contributor economics must be disclosed and accepted before that monetisation begins.

Public submissions. The alpha currently records claims as public GitHub issues. Do not include information you would not want published. GitHub's own terms and privacy practices also apply.
