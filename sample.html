Lead 01

The World War II prison break that became a mass death event.

At about 2 a.m. on 5 August 1944, Japanese prisoners at Cowra, Australia, charged barbed wire and machine-gun positions with improvised weapons after a bugle signal. Hundreds escaped, but the breakout became one of the bloodiest prison escapes of the war.

Why it worksA secret vote, an unauthorised bugle, improvised weapons, burning huts and a breakout many participants could not realistically expect to survive.
Suggested angleWhy would hundreds of prisoners attempt an escape they were unlikely to survive?
Sources:
Australian War Memorial
National Archives of Australia
UncertaintyOfficial sources differ slightly on the Japanese death count. A responsible script should identify the figure and source used.
Lead 02

The dead rat that helped change how Britain preserves history.

In 1836, civil servant Henry Cole gave evidence about the appalling condition of Britain’s public records and physically produced vermin remains before a Parliamentary committee. The National Archives still keeps rat remains associated with that episode.

Why it worksOne grotesque physical artifact opens into the history of how a modern national archive was created.
Suggested angleThe dead rat that helped change how Britain preserves history.
Sources:
The National Archives — Sir Henry Cole’s rat
UncertaintyThe rat did not single-handedly create the Public Record Office; it was vivid evidence within a broader reform campaign.
Lead 03

The alleged plan to rescue Napoleon with primitive submarines.

During Napoleon’s exile on St Helena, British smuggler and adventurer Tom Johnson claimed he had been offered £40,000 to help rescue the former emperor using primitive submarines and a cliff-lowering system.

Why it worksThe audience begins with an apparently absurd claim, then discovers that the machinery was more real than expected while the full plot remains uncertain.
Suggested angleThe Napoleon submarine rescue plot: brilliant plan, self-promotion, or both?
Sources:
Smithsonian Magazine
U.S. Naval Institute Proceedings
UncertaintyDo not present the rescue attempt as an established executed operation. Evidence for the machinery is stronger than evidence for the complete operational rescue scenario.
